I-Init: IMU-Only Initialization for Learning Inertial Odometry

Abstract

State initialization is essential for robotic navigation and simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM). Conventional initialization methods fuse inertial measurements with exteroceptive sensors, such as cameras and LiDAR. However, these sensors can become unreliable under aggressive motion or poor geometric conditions. Inertial odometry (IO) estimates motion using only inertial measurement units (IMUs), eliminating dependence on exteroceptive sensors. However, existing learning-based IO methods typically assume access to ground-truth initial states, leaving the initialization problem largely unexplored. This assumption limits their applicability to real-world deployment. To bridge this gap, we present I-Init, an IMU-only initialization method that estimates the initial velocity, gravity direction, and gyroscope bias for learning-based inertial odometry. I-Init leverages a unified Transformer-based Motion Network for both initialization and odometry. The network predictions provide constraints for initial state estimation without exteroceptive sensors. On EuRoC, I-Init achieves comparable gravity-direction estimation accuracy to the visual-inertial VINS-Mono initializer, with a 95% initialization success rate compared to 51% for VINS-Mono. Experiments on EuRoC, Blackbird, and TLIO demonstrate state-of-the-art inertial odometry performance. We demonstrate that odometry initialized with our IMU-only initialization is very close to the ground-truth initialization.

Methods

I-Init has three components:

  1. Motion Network. Predicts body-frame velocity, roll & pitch, and their uncertainties from raw IMU. It needs no attitude input, so it works from the first window.
  2. IMU-only initializer. Aligns these predictions with IMU preintegration to estimate Xinit={gb0, vb0b0, bg}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{init}} = \{\mathbf{g}^{b_0},\ \mathbf{v}_{b_0}^{b_0},\ \mathbf{b}_g\}Xinit={gb0​, vb0​b0​​, bg​}.
  3. Uncertainty-aware EKF. Fuses IMU propagation with the network's velocity, weighted by the predicted covariance.

One network, two window lengths: the convolutional stem (kernel 16, stride 8) gives one token per key-frame and accepts any input length, so the same weights serve the 1024-sample odometry windows and the shorter initialization windows.

Evaluation

We compare IMU-only with visual-inertial initialization on EuRoC, and odometry under IMU-only versus oracle (ground-truth) initialization on three datasets. IMU-only runs use no ground truth at inference.

  • EuRoC

    Micro aerial vehicle. Evaluated on MH02, MH04, V101, V103, and V202.

  • Blackbird

    Aggressive indoor quadrotor flights. 75 training, 9 test, and 10 evaluation flights.

  • TLIO

    Head-mounted device, pedestrian motion. Published split and evaluation protocol.

Finding 1: IMU-only initialization can be more reliable than visual-inertial initialization

2.5 s EuRoC segments. Success: gravity-direction error below 5° and velocity RMSE below 0.5 m/s.

  • I-Init:+rp with AirIMU succeeds on 95% of segments versus 51% for VINS-Mono, with lower gravity error (1.445° vs. 1.611°).
  • Above 40°/s, VINS-Mono succeeds on at most 12% of segments per cell; I-Init on 62–100%.

Note: VINS-Mono has lower velocity RMSE (0.169 vs. 0.267 m/s). I-Init is least reliable above 1.1 m/s.

Finding 2: Gyroscope bias is the dominant error source of IMU-only alignment

An uncompensated bias corrupts the preintegrated rotation and distorts the gravity estimate.

  • With raw IMU, vel succeeds on only 13% of segments, below Network Init (25%).
  • +rp estimates the bias, halving the gravity error (6.05° to 3.04°) and raising success to 86%. Its bias-magnitude error is 4.63%, versus 7.18% for VINS-Mono.
  • With AirIMU, vel and +rp both reach 95%.

Note: We use T = 2.5 s: shorter windows carry little motion, and with AirIMU the velocity error and success rate level off around 2.5–3.0 s.

Finding 3: IMU-only initialization preserves odometry accuracy

The goal is to match the same EKF under oracle initialization. IMU-only runs are aligned only in initial heading and position.

  • With I-Init:+rp, ATE stays within 0.07 m of oracle initialization on all three datasets.
  • Refinement lowers ATE relative to Network Init everywhere, e.g. 3.06 to 2.91 m on EuRoC.
  • Without attitude input, our EKF has the lowest oracle ATE on EuRoC and TLIO.

Note: AirIO EKF is more accurate on Blackbird (2.33 vs. 2.76 m).

Finding 4: Learned uncertainty is essential for fusing learned velocity with IMU propagation

The Motion Network's R and AirIMU's Q are each set to identity or to their learned values; nothing else changes.

  • Learning both gives the lowest ATE and AVE on every dataset.
  • Learned Q with identity R raises both errors everywhere, up to 59.6 m ATE on Blackbird.

Note: The filter weighs velocity against propagation by the ratio of R to Q, so both must be learned.
