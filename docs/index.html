I-Init: IMU-Only Initialization for Learning Inertial Odometry

Abstract

State initialization is essential for robotic navigation and simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM). Conventional initialization methods fuse inertial measurements with exteroceptive sensors, such as cameras and LiDAR. However, these sensors can become unreliable under aggressive motion or poor geometric conditions. Inertial odometry (IO) estimates motion using only inertial measurement units (IMUs), eliminating dependence on exteroceptive sensors. However, existing learning-based IO methods typically assume access to ground-truth initial states, leaving the initialization problem largely unexplored. This assumption limits their applicability to real-world deployment. To bridge this gap, we present I-Init, an IMU-only initialization method that estimates the initial velocity, gravity direction, and gyroscope bias for learning-based inertial odometry. I-Init leverages a unified Transformer-based Motion Network for both initialization and odometry. The network predictions provide constraints for initial state estimation without exteroceptive sensors. On EuRoC, I-Init achieves comparable gravity-direction estimation accuracy to the visual-inertial VINS-Mono initializer, with a 95% initialization success rate compared to 51% for VINS-Mono. Experiments on EuRoC, Blackbird, and TLIO demonstrate state-of-the-art inertial odometry performance. We demonstrate that odometry initialized with our IMU-only initialization is very close to the ground-truth initialization.

Initialization Without Exteroceptive Sensors

Methods

I-Init estimates motion from IMU measurements alone with three components:

  1. Motion Network. A Transformer predicts body-frame velocity, roll & pitch, and their uncertainties from a window of raw IMU samples. It takes no attitude or other state estimate as input, so it operates from the first IMU window onward.
  2. IMU-only initializer. Aligning the network's predictions with IMU preintegration estimates the gravity direction, initial velocity, and gyroscope bias, Xinit={gb0, vb0b0, bg}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{init}} = \{\mathbf{g}^{b_0},\ \mathbf{v}_{b_0}^{b_0},\ \mathbf{b}_g\}Xinit={gb0​, vb0​b0​​, bg​}.
  3. Uncertainty-aware EKF. The estimated state initializes an EKF that fuses IMU propagation with velocity predictions from the same network, weighted by their predicted covariance.

Velocity alignment

An IMU alone cannot determine the initial velocity or the gravity direction, because integration propagates both from unknown initial values. The network supplies the missing reference: each velocity prediction is computed from a window of measurements rather than accumulated by integration, so its error does not grow with time. With AirIMU correction, the learned corrections already compensate the gyroscope bias, so the residual bias stays fixed at zero.

One network, two window lengths: a residual one-dimensional convolution with kernel size 16 and stride 8 embeds 1024 six-channel IMU samples into 127 tokens, one per key-frame, followed by 6 pre-norm attention blocks with 6 heads each and four two-layer MLP heads of width 128. Because the convolutional stem and the sinusoidal encodings accept inputs of any length, the same weights process both the 1024-sample odometry windows and the shorter initialization windows.

Evaluation

We compare IMU-only initialization with visual-inertial initialization on EuRoC, and test whether odometry with IMU-only initialization retains the accuracy of the same EKF under oracle initialization on three datasets. Oracle initialization supplies the ground-truth initial state; IMU-only initialization estimates it with I-Init and uses no ground-truth input at inference.

  • EuRoC

    Micro aerial vehicle. Evaluated on MH02, MH04, V101, V103, and V202.

  • Blackbird

    Aggressive indoor quadrotor flights. 75 training, 9 test, and 10 evaluation flights.

  • TLIO

    Head-mounted device, pedestrian motion. Published split and evaluation protocol.

Finding 1: IMU-only initialization can be more reliable than visual-inertial initialization

We evaluate on 2.5 s segments from the EuRoC evaluation sequences. A segment succeeds when the gravity-direction error is below 5° and the velocity RMSE is below 0.5 m/s.

  • I-Init:+rp with AirIMU correction succeeds on 95% of segments, compared with 51% for VINS-Mono, with a lower average gravity-direction error (1.445° versus 1.611°).
  • Above 40°/s, VINS-Mono succeeds on at most 12% of the segments in any cell and on none above 55°/s; in the same cells, I-Init succeeds on 62–100%.
  • VINS-Mono also fails on most near-stationary segments (9% below 0.1 m/s), where I-Init succeeds on 90%.

Note: VINS-Mono attains a lower velocity RMSE (0.169 versus 0.267 m/s). I-Init is least reliable above 1.1 m/s, dropping to 57–75% in several cells and to 0% in the cell combining 1.3–2.7 m/s with 30–40°/s, in which VINS-Mono also fails.

Finding 2: Gyroscope bias is the dominant error source of IMU-only alignment

An uncompensated bias corrupts the preintegrated rotation, which misaligns the predicted velocities and distorts the gravity estimate. Whether learned by AirIMU or estimated by +rp, bias compensation is essential.

  • With raw IMU, vel succeeds on only 13% of segments, fewer than the unrefined Network Init (25%).
  • Estimating the bias through the roll & pitch residuals halves the gravity-direction error, from 6.048° to 3.042°, and raises the success rate to 86%. The estimated bias magnitude deviates from the reference by 4.63%, compared with 7.18% for VINS-Mono.
  • With AirIMU correction, vel and +rp both reach 95% success, so the roll & pitch residuals add little once the bias is compensated.

Note: The window length trades motion information against accumulated bias. At T = 0.5 s, gravity-direction errors are approximately 4–5°; with AirIMU correction, the velocity error and success rate saturate around T = 2.5–3.0 s. We therefore use T = 2.5 s as a practical compromise between initialization delay and accuracy.

Finding 3: IMU-only initialization preserves odometry accuracy

The goal is not to outperform the oracle, but to match the same EKF under oracle initialization. IMU-only runs are aligned only in initial heading and position, with no trajectory fitting, scale correction, or roll & pitch alignment.

  • Odometry with I-Init:+rp reaches an ATE of 2.91, 2.79, and 1.35 m on EuRoC, Blackbird, and TLIO, within 0.07 m of oracle initialization on every dataset.
  • Refinement reduces the ATE relative to Network Init on all three datasets, for example from 3.06 to 2.91 m on EuRoC.
  • Without attitude input, the Motion Network remains competitive: among oracle-initialized EKF variants, ours attains the lowest ATE on EuRoC (2.97 m) and TLIO (1.35 m).

Note: AirIO EKF remains more accurate on Blackbird (2.33 versus 2.76 m). Without an EKF, I-Init attains the lowest ATE on all three datasets, while AirIO attains a lower AVE on EuRoC (0.286 versus 0.360 m/s).

Finding 4: Learned uncertainty is essential for fusing learned velocity with IMU propagation

We independently set the measurement-noise covariance R of the Motion Network and the propagation-noise covariance Q of AirIMU to identity matrices or to their learned values. All other components remain fixed, so the comparison isolates the effect of uncertainty weighting.

  • Using learned values for both covariances yields the lowest ATE and AVE on all three datasets.
  • Learning only one does not consistently help: learned Q with identity R increases both errors on every dataset, reaching an ATE of 59.6 m on Blackbird.

Note: The filter balances velocity measurements against IMU propagation through the relative scale of R and Q, so the two covariances are effective only when both are learned.

Limitations

  • As in visual-inertial odometry, global yaw and position remain unobservable.
  • Over a short window, the accelerometer bias is coupled with gravity and is therefore not estimated.
  • Estimating all three gyroscope-bias components requires rotation within the window.
  • The learned motion may degrade outside the training distribution.

Future work will investigate cross-platform generalization of the Motion Network and the stability of EKF fusion with learned velocity measurements.
